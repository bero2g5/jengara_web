AI Investment Diligence Checklist

By Jengara · Practical working guide

AI diligence should connect technical evidence to an investment decision. This checklist organizes seven questions around the investment thesis, operating model and actions required after close. Complete it with the investment lead and technical reviewers. Request primary evidence, record contradictions and distinguish what is supported from what cannot be verified within the diligence window.

Begin by naming the transaction, investment thesis and accountable lead. Identify the material AI claim: which revenue, retention, margin or operating-leverage assumption depends on AI? Define what would change valuation, deal protection, timing or willingness to proceed. These thresholds help keep the review focused on material findings.

1. Is AI material to the investment thesis?

Request product usage, customer evidence, retention analysis and revenue or cost attribution. Separate observed results from forecasts. Trace the dependency between each AI claim and the financial model. A demonstration can show a capability, but the finding should explain what evidence supports the business assumption and what remains unverified.

2. Is the data advantage durable and permissible?

Inspect data lineage, permissions, contractual restrictions, refresh processes and quality measures. Ask where evaluation data came from and whether it represents the intended workload. Consider how easily a competitor could reproduce or substitute the claimed advantage. Record limitations as well as strengths, including the owner of any follow-up evidence request.

3. Can the architecture operate at forecast scale?

Review production measurements, latency distribution, failure logs, load-test conditions, recovery exercises and dependency maps. Distinguish production observations from prototype estimates. State the workload actually tested, the measured limits and the gap to the forecast. Where remediation is needed, record an owner and cost range rather than assuming scaling will be straightforward.

4. Do the economics survive adoption?

Request a fully loaded cost per accepted business outcome. Include model use, retrieval, retries, tool calls, controls, human review and platform operation. Record the acceptance rate, cost boundary and allocation method. Test downside usage and quality scenarios, then explain how those assumptions affect margin. Define what must be demonstrated before accepting a growth or margin assumption.

5. Is concentration risk understood?

Map dependencies on model vendors, clouds, key people, proprietary data and geographic capacity. Examine switching time, transition cost, potential quality loss and shared failure domains. A named alternative is only useful when the practical transition constraints are understood. Connect each material dependency to a decision consequence and an accountable next action.

6. Are controls proportionate to the use case?

Inspect the system inventory, accountability, access, evaluation, monitoring, incident response and human oversight. Record the control tested, evidence inspected and remaining gap. Qualified reviewers should assess obligations for the actual deployment context. The checklist organizes evidence; it does not make a jurisdiction-specific legal determination.

7. What must happen after close?

Separate deal protections, first-100-day priorities and longer-term platform investment. Attach an owner, cost range, timing and evidence gate to each material issue. Define completion in terms of evidence, so that a post-close action can be accepted or challenged consistently.

Turn findings into an investment recommendation

Use the same finding record throughout: claim, evidence inspected, limitation, impact, owner and timing. Classify the conclusion as supported, conditional, contradicted or unverified. Missing evidence should remain visible rather than becoming a positive conclusion.

Summarize the material findings in the investment memo section. Choose proceed, proceed with conditions, obtain evidence or do not proceed, and explain the reasoning. List unresolved assumptions, their materiality and who accepts the residual uncertainty. Keep the evidence trail with the completed checklist so that the investment committee and post-close owners can follow the recommendation.
