From tokens to gross margin: measuring the real economics of enterprise AI

By Frank Ribeiro, Jengara Advisory · 26 August 2026 · Research and executive analysis

The financially relevant AI metric is not cost per token. It is the fully loaded cost of producing a successful, valuable and controlled business outcome.

Research approach

Original Jengara operating model informed by the FinOps Foundation’s unit-economics and AI cost-management guidance. No vendor price is used because model and accelerator pricing changes frequently.

Begin with the business unit

Select a unit that management already understands: a resolved support case, approved claim, completed workflow, generated design accepted by a user or revenue-producing interaction. This prevents inexpensive but unusable output from appearing efficient.

Build a complete cost boundary

Allocate model inference, embeddings, retrieval, vector storage, orchestration, external tools, observability, security controls, accelerator capacity and human review. Include failed attempts and abandoned runs; they consume resources without creating the target outcome.

Measure goodput, not raw throughput

Throughput counts completed requests. Goodput counts outputs that meet the required quality, latency and control threshold. A cheaper model with more retries or lower acceptance can have a higher cost per successful outcome.

Segment workloads before optimizing

Interactive, batch, reasoning and autonomous-agent workloads have different quality and latency requirements. Model routing, caching and smaller-model substitution should be evaluated within each segment rather than applied uniformly.

Connect consumption to gross margin

Translate workload forecasts into cost per customer, transaction or product tier. Test adoption, prompt growth, context-window growth, retry rate and vendor-pricing cases. The purpose is to show when AI consumption becomes margin accretive, neutral or dilutive.

Create financial and engineering controls

Assign budget ownership, allocation rules, evaluation thresholds and exception processes. Engineering should own technical efficiency; product should own outcome quality; finance should govern unit economics. None can manage AI value independently.

Jengara decision rule

Optimize for the lowest risk-adjusted cost per successful business outcome—not the lowest token price, smallest model or highest benchmark score.

Executive takeaway

The quality of an AI decision depends on linking technical evidence to economics, controls, operating capacity and accountable action.
